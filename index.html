⛴️Getting here & checking in

Monhegan has no cars. You'll reach the island by ferry and get around on foot. Pack light, and in soft bags if you can: soft duffels or bins ride the open ferry deck better than hard suitcases, and a trash bag tucked inside keeps things dry if the crossing turns wet. Book your crossing ahead: the Monhegan Boat Line requires advance reservations, and the other two lines strongly recommend them in summer. The boat will not wait for you, and midcoast traffic runs slow in July and August, so pad your drive.

Finding the house (about a 10 minute walk)

Magnolia House is at 2 Cabot Road, an easy walk south from the ferry dock, roughly a third of a mile.

  1. You'll land at the ferry dock. The Barnacle café is right there at the wharf.
  2. Walk up the hill from the wharf to the main road and follow it south, inland and away from the harbor, through the village. You'll pass the church, the post office, and the Monhegan House hotel.
  3. Keep going up a second, smaller hill for about ten minutes total (a third of a mile), until you see the sign for the Trailing Yew (the inn) on your right.
  4. Just past the Trailing Yew buildings, a little side road opens on the right; that's Cabot Road, and Magnolia House is the very next house, right across it: grey-shingled, with a big picture window.

🏠The house & staying comfortable

Heat. The heater in the living room works beautifully. Please be mindful of both electricity and propane, and don't leave it (or the lights) running when you don't need them.

Fans and extra warmth. There are fans throughout the house, and space heaters if you'd like extra warmth in a room.

Cooling. A couple of the upstairs bedrooms have portable air-conditioning units for hot, still afternoons. As with everything here, please run them only when you need them and switch them off when you leave the room.

Bathrooms. The house's only shower is in the downstairs bathroom; the upstairs bath is a half-bath (toilet and sink). Worth knowing when a full house is sharing mornings.

Hot water comes from a propane heater. Propane is very expensive and difficult to transport to the island, one more reason to keep showers short.

Kitchen. The gas range lights electronically: turn the knob to Light and it clicks and catches. Please make sure the fridge and freezer doors close tightly, and be gentle with the drawers. You're welcome to use anything already in the kitchen: pantry staples, condiments, spices, and whatever earlier guests have left.

Fireplace. If you light a fire, keep the screen in front of it and never leave it unattended. Flattened cardboard by the fireplace makes good kindling.

Firewood. We usually keep the house stocked, but deliveries have been a challenge this year, so it's smart to pick up a couple of bundles on your way (a store-bought bundle rides the boat just fine). You're welcome to use whatever you find at the house, and driftwood works great too.

Power. The island generates its own electricity, so it's precious, and it can blip in storms. When it drops, the Wi-Fi goes with it until it's back. A flashlight lives on the open shelf beside the kitchen stove, and there are more lights in the utility closet.

In a hard, wind-driven rain, the upstairs sliding door can take a little water into its channel. Lay a towel in the track (spares are in that bedroom's closet) and let me know.

The basement is just mechanicals and storage: steep old stairs, low beams, and nothing you'll need, so please skip it (kids especially).

💧 Water, please read (water conservation is essential for the house to stay open for guests)

The house runs on town water plus a cistern, and the septic system is still recovering from heavy use. On a small island, water is scarce, so a few easy habits make all the difference:

  • Keep showers short. A quick in-and-out.
  • Turn the tap off while brushing teeth or soaping up.
  • Please don't leave the water running while you do the dishes.
  • Flush only human waste and toilet paper.
Please DO NOT flush (even if it says "flushable"): wipes of any kind · paper towels or tissues · feminine or hygiene products · cotton balls or swabs · dental floss · food, grease, or coffee grounds · anything that isn't toilet paper.

There is no usable washer or dryer at the house this season. The washing machine is offline while the septic recovers, and there's no public laundromat on the island this year either. Please bring enough clothing for your stay, plus your own bed linens and bath towels. (For small loads there's a hand-wash kit, a small tub, washboard, scrubber, and soap. A clothesline with clothespins is in the side yard, and there's a drying rack for swimsuits and towels.)

If you do need laundry on a longer stay, one setup works for a car-free island:

  • Park Street Laundromat & Dry Cleaners (Rockland), (207) 594-9393. Wash-dry-fold, and in season they offer free pickup and delivery to Port Clyde and Tenants Harbor, the Monhegan Boat Line's landing. Call a few days ahead and say it's for Monhegan.
  • Your bag rides the boat. The Monhegan Boat Line's freight service carries laundry to and from the mainland, and an island trucker (Monhegan Trucking, (207) 975-5833) can move it between the house and the dock. Label it with your name and "Magnolia House, Monhegan."
  • Easiest: call Park Street (207-594-9393) and the Monhegan Boat Line ((207) 372-8848) a few days before your trip to confirm the pickup day, cost, and boat timing, and they'll arrange the hand-off between them.

Curious how much your habits add up to? Try the Monhegan Water Conservation Calculator. A water-wise group of six saves about a thousand gallons over a week. It matters that much.

Running toilet or dripping faucet? Text me right away, even a small leak drains the system fast. Thank you, this is the single most important thing for keeping the house happy.

♻️Trash & recycling

Everything on Monhegan has to leave the island by boat, so the island uses a specific bag system. Please follow it; it matters.

  • Trash goes in the town's official trash bags, the red ones. Buy them at the Monhegan Store (just ask for trash bags). A large bag is about $12. All trash goes in these.
  • Put trash out on Fridays and Mondays.
  • Recycling goes in BLUE bags (in the cabinet next to the fridge). Please rinse everything first.
  • Cardboard and paper: either flatten it and leave it by the fireplace for kindling, or pick up recycling stickers at the store.
  • The bins are at the side of the house.
  • Extra bags for the indoor trash cans are in the cabinet by the fridge, or in the closets near the bathrooms.
  • Please don't leave food trash outside overnight unless it's in the bins, the island has plenty of hungry critters.

✅Before you check out: the cleaning routine

Check-out is 10:00 AM, and please be prompt. Someone will do a walkthrough between guests to make sure the house is clean and ready. Instead of charging a cleaning fee, we (like most rentals on Monhegan) ask guests to leave the house clean and ready for the family arriving behind you. If you'd like help arranging a cleaner, let me know and we can connect you with someone on the island who can help. If additional cleaning is needed after checkout, cleaning fees will be deducted from the security/cleaning deposit. Here's the routine:

Kitchen

  • Dishes, pots, and pans washed, dried, and back in their places
  • Fridge cleared of your food and drinks, and given a quick wipe
  • Stove spills wiped up, cabinet fronts and counters wiped down
  • Leave behind only food that's helpful to the next guests (condiments, unexpired eggs or butter); everything else goes with the trash

Bathrooms

  • Wipe down the sink, shower, and toilet bowl
  • Take your toiletries, and your towels with your linens

Bedrooms & living areas

  • Strip the beds and pack your own linens and towels (leave mattress pads and comforter covers on)
  • Fold the house blankets and leave them on the beds
  • Shake out the small rugs, then vacuum or sweep, and mop the floors (the vacuum's on the main floor). If the mop's wet, leave it on the deck or the rack behind the basement door, not in the closet
  • If you used the fireplace, please sweep out the ashes (cold ashes only, into a trash bag)
  • Furniture and kitchen items back where you found them
  • If you brought a pet: vacuum and wipe down thoroughly, inside and out, so there's no trace

Trash & outside

  • Bag the trash correctly (red for trash, blue for recycling) and take it to the side bins
  • Sweep off the decks

On your way out

  • Windows closed, skylights too
  • Heat, lights, and fans off
  • Check drawers and closets for personal belongings, and for chargers, cords, and anything under the beds
  • Bags out for Monhegan Trucking to collect, if you've arranged pickup with Mel and Stew (they'll tell you the time for your boat)
  • Front door pulled shut on your way out (there's no key, it stays unlocked; please don't lock anything)

Cleaning supplies live under the kitchen sink and in the bathrooms; mops and brooms might be by the basement door or right outside on the deck. If something ran out, or something broke or needs attention, just text me. I'd always rather know.

🩺 Medical care on Monhegan

There's no doctor's office, pharmacy, or hospital on the island, so it pays to know how care works here before you need it:

  • In any emergency, dial 911. It works on Monhegan; the call is dispatched through the county center, and the island's trained volunteer responders come to you.
  • For minor things (sprains, cuts, rashes, coughs, allergies), the volunteer-run Monhegan First Aid Station on the main road (you'll have passed it on the walk to the house) operates roughly late May to mid-October, with walk-in hours Mon-Sat 9-10 AM and Mon-Fri 4-5 PM. It's free (donations welcome) and staffed by licensed volunteer providers. It can't manage chronic conditions or renew prescriptions.
  • Serious emergencies are evacuated to the mainland, by LifeFlight helicopter, Coast Guard boat, or ferry-to-ambulance, depending on weather. Help arrives, but it takes longer than it would on the mainland; keep that in mind on the cliffs and in the water.
  • Nearest 24/7 emergency rooms (both MaineHealth): Pen Bay Hospital, 6 Glen Cove Dr, Rockport, (207) 301-8315, on the Port Clyde side; Lincoln Hospital, 35 Miles St, Damariscotta, (207) 563-4521, on the New Harbor side.
  • Walk-in care on the mainland: MaineHealth Walk-In Care, 75 Maverick St, Rockland, (207) 301-6000, daily 8 AM to 6 PM; MaineHealth Urgent Care, 6 St. Andrews Ln, Boothbay Harbor, (207) 633-1940.
  • Pharmacies are mainland-only: Walgreens in Rockland (207) 596-0036 or Damariscotta (207) 563-3506. Bring every prescription you need, and your EpiPen if you use one.
  • Ticks & poison ivy: Monhegan removed its deer in the 1990s, so tick risk is far lower than mainland Maine, but standard precautions still apply on the trails: repellent, long sleeves, and a tick check after hiking. Poison ivy grows trailside too.

Exploring Monhegan

🚶Getting around

On foot, always. The village is compact and distances are short, but the roads are unpaved and the terrain is hilly, rocky, and often muddy. Wear sturdy shoes, and because there are no street lights, carry a flashlight after dark. The only wheels at the dock are for luggage. If you need a ride, or a lift for someone who can't manage the walk, Monhegan Cab Company runs a multi-seat golf cart (207-975-6190) and also gives scenic island tours.

🥾Hiking & trails

Some nine miles of marked trails lace the island's wildlands, most of them moderate to challenging as they climb through forest to the eastern headlands and run along the shore. They're cared for by Monhegan Associates, the local land trust. Trails are a numbered system (white numbers on small green wood blocks, plus rock cairns over the ledges). Pick up the current printed trail map when you arrive, sold for a nominal fee at the ferry ticket booths and village shops. Carry it once you leave the village. Trail rules: no fires, no smoking, no camping, no bikes; the wildlands are a serious fire hazard.

Not to miss

  • Cliff Trail (#1): the headland loop along the highest ocean cliffs on the Maine coast. The signature Monhegan hike.
  • Cathedral Woods (#11): a hushed spruce-and-fir forest, and the home of the beloved fairy houses, tiny dwellings of sticks, moss, and stones along the trail. Keep an eye out for them. A must with kids.
  • Whitehead: village streets rise to sheer ~150-foot cliffs with wide open-ocean views.
  • Lobster Cove: an easy shoreline walk to the rusted wreck of the tugboat D.T. Sheridan (aground since 1948).
  • Burnt Head: a beautiful headland trail, and in late summer the path out to it is loaded with wild blackberries. Wear long sleeves and bring a container.
  • Lighthouse Hill: a short uphill to the 1824 lighthouse, the museum, and the best harbor overlook.
Please be careful near the water. Never swim or wade at Lobster Cove or anywhere on the back side: the undertows are unpredictable. Big "comber" waves arrive without warning even on calm days (a storm far out to sea is enough), and the black, moss-stained rocks near the surf are treacherously slick; people have been lost from them. Keep a bulwark between you and the surf, stay well above the tide line, and keep children close. And watch for ticks and poison ivy on the trails.

🎨See & do

  • Monhegan Museum of Art & History at the 1824 light station on Lighthouse Hill: island history, art, and the keeper's house museum. Open June 15 to Sept 30 this year: 11:30 AM-3:30 PM daily in high summer (12:30-2:30 in the June and late-September shoulders). Adults $10, kids & students $5, family $20.
  • The island library (Monhegan Memorial Library) is a cozy, quiet spot to browse and read. Check its posted hours.
  • Open studios. Monhegan has been an artists' colony for over a century, and many painters open their studios on set days. Pick up the printed Monhegan Artists Studio Map (it lists open hours) at the Wharf Shed or Rope Shed when you land, or at the galleries: Lupine Gallery (48 Main St, daily 11-4:30 in season, art supplies too) and Winter Works, the islanders' craft co-op on Wharf Hill Rd.
  • Fairy houses in Cathedral Woods (see Hiking): keep an eye out for the tiny stick-and-moss houses along the trail.
  • Check the bulletin boards (one by the grocery store, one on the shed) and the island's online events calendar for current happenings: talks, concerts, church suppers, and open-studio days.
  • Swim Beach near the dock is the main swimming spot, and you can also swim at Fish Beach. The water is cold (around 60°F even in high summer), the beaches are small, and the tides are strong.
  • Kayaking: there's no formal rental shop on the island, but kayaks are sometimes available at Fish Beach (Monhegan Kayak Rentals, 207-701-7736; they book only a day or so ahead, no website). Please take the island's own advice seriously: paddling around Monhegan is hazardous: stay inside the harbor, never paddle alone, wear the PFD, and remember the water is about 60°F. For a real day of paddling, rent on the mainland instead: Port Clyde Kayaks (207-372-8100, in the ferry village; kayaks and guided tours on Muscongus Bay) or Maine Sport Outfitters in Rockport (207-236-8797).
  • Tide pools along the rocky shore, and world-class birding, especially during fall migration (try Ice Pond and the Meadow).
  • Manana Island, the little island across the harbor, with its historic inscriptions. There's no scheduled shuttle, but a skiff ride across can usually be arranged at Fish Beach or through one of the inns.

🤝 Give back: the island community (MISCA)

Monhegan is a real year-round community, not only a summer destination, and keeping it that way takes real work. The Monhegan Island Sustainable Community Association (MISCA) is the nonprofit that makes year-round island life possible. It buys homes on the island and keeps them affordable for the families who live here all year, which is the single best way to help Monhegan stay a living, working, vibrant community.

If your time here meant something to you, please consider giving back. There's a contribution jar on the table next to the fireplace, and you can donate online at miscainfo.com/donate. Gifts are tax-deductible. Thank you.

🧭Good to know

  • Bring cash. Some spots are cash-only, and when the internet drops, cards don't work. There are only two ATMs (The Barnacle and The Novelty) and no bank.
  • Cell service is spotty, usable near the village and poor on the back side; Verizon tends to fare best. Plan to be largely offline.
  • Medical: no pharmacy or hospital on the island; see Medical care on Monhegan for what to do, and bring your own prescriptions and any EpiPen.
  • Restrooms are limited in the village, so use the ferry's facilities before you land.
  • Weather is cool, damp, and often foggy even in summer. Pack layers, a windbreaker, and rain gear, plus sturdy shoes. Since shoes come off at the door, add slippers or warm socks for the house.
  • Sun and bugs: bring sunscreen and insect repellent. The beaches and open headlands get full sun, and mosquitoes can be thick near the house in summer, so keep the screen doors shut behind you.
  • On the fridge: a Monhegan area map and a handwritten list of island business hours, a handy quick reference when you're planning the day.
  • Leave no trace. Monhegan runs carry-in, carry-out. Stay on trails, pack out what you bring, and keep pets leashed (Maine law).
